Before I let this go...

I don't know if there is ever a perfect way to say goodbye to someone you once imagined staying.

Maybe there isn't.

Maybe all we can really do is be grateful for what existed, accept what didn't, and carry the memories without asking them to become something they were never meant to be.

A letter I never thought I'd have to write

Dear Aubrey/Langga,

I spent a long time wondering how our story would end. Somewhere inside me, I think I always hoped it wouldn't.

I imagined more conversations, more laughter, more ordinary days that would slowly become important simply because you were part of them.

But sometimes two people can mean something to each other and still not be meant to continue walking the same road.

And I think that is the hardest kind of ending to accept.

Because there is no villain here. No great betrayal. No moment I can point to and say, "That is where everything went wrong."

There was simply a point where what I hoped for and what reality could give me became two different things.

For a while, I wanted to fight against that. I wanted effort to be enough. I wanted patience to be enough. I wanted the way I cared about you to somehow be enough to rewrite the ending.

But love cannot be held together by one person refusing to let go.

So this isn't me asking you to reconsider.

This isn't another attempt to convince you that we could have been something beautiful.

Maybe we could have.

But that possibility belongs to another version of our story now.

What I want to say instead is thank you.

Thank you for every conversation that made an ordinary night feel a little less ordinary.

Thank you for the moments when seeing your name appear on my screen was enough to make me smile.

Thank you for reminding me that I could still care about someone deeply, even when caring eventually came with the responsibility of letting them go.

I won't pretend it doesn't hurt.

Some part of me will probably always wonder what would have happened if the timing had been different, if circumstances had been kinder, or if we had met each other at another point in our lives.

But I don't want those questions to turn something beautiful into something bitter.

Whatever we were — and whatever we almost became — mattered to me.

And because it mattered, I want to remember it gently.

I hope life becomes everything you are searching for. I hope you find peace in places where you once felt uncertain. I hope you become the person you've always wanted to become.

And someday, if my name ever crosses your mind, I hope you don't remember someone who couldn't let go.

I hope you remember someone who cared about you sincerely, who was grateful that our paths crossed, and who eventually loved you enough to stop asking you to stay.

So this is where I leave our story.

Not erased.

Not regretted.

Just finished.

And wherever life takes us after this, I hope it is somewhere good.

Goodbye,

— Bernard
